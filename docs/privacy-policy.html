Privacy policy

  • App: Steps GPS Route
  • Developer: bukin
  • Contact: dev.mechanics998877@gmail.com
  • Effective date: 2026-11-02

This policy explains what the app does with your information. Steps GPS Route shows how much you walk each day, and records GPS routes of trips you start yourself, such as hikes, nature walks or off-road drives, so you can view them on a map and export them as GPX files.

Steps GPS Route is not a health or medical app. It gives no health advice, and it is not meant to diagnose, treat or track any health condition.

Summary

  • The app reads only your step count from Health Connect, and uses your location only while a trip you started is recording or while you have its map open.
  • Your steps, trips, the routes you import and your settings are stored only on your device. The app has no account, no analytics and no crash reporting, and it does not send your information to the developer. It shows ads from Google AdMob on its Stats, History and Trips screens; Google's ad software gets none of your steps, trips, routes or GPS location (see Ads below).
  • The app's internet traffic is loading the map when you open a trip's screen or the app's map (the map from OpenFreeMap and its hill shading from Mapterhorn), downloading an offline map from GitHub if you ask for one, and Google's ads (see Ads below). Your GPS points are not sent.
  • A trip leaves your device only when you save or share its GPX file yourself. A GPX file you import is read on your device, with no internet.

Step data from Health Connect

If you allow it, the app reads your step count from Health Connect, Android's on-device store for activity data. It asks only for permission to read steps and does not read any other kind of data.

  • What is read. The number of steps in each minute. Health Connect combines the steps counted by your phone with steps from any other app or device you've connected to Health Connect. The app reads only this combined total, not which app or device recorded the steps. On first use, or after a long break, it reads up to the last 30 days.
  • When. Only while the app is open. It never reads steps in the background.
  • What it's used for. Showing your daily and weekly steps, your progress toward your step goal, your step history, lifetime stats, and the split into workout and incidental steps.
  • What happens to it. A copy of the step counts is stored in the app's private storage on your device. The app never writes anything to Health Connect, never sends step data off your device, and never sells or shares it, uses it for advertising, or uses it for any purpose other than the features above. It is never passed to the app's ad software.

The use of information received from Health Connect adheres to the Health Connect Permissions policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

You can withdraw the permission at any time in Health Connect's settings. The app then stops reading steps; steps it already copied stay in the app until you uninstall it.

Location and GPS trips

The app uses your location only in these two cases, both started by you:

  • While a trip you started is recording. The app asks for precise location when you start (or resume) a trip.
  • While the app's map is open (the Map tab), to show where you are on the map. Approximate location is enough for this. The app stops asking for your location as soon as you leave the map or switch to another app. Locations used by the map are only shown: they are not stored, and they are not added to any trip. The map's compass, which shows which way the phone points and can turn the map with you, reads the phone's motion and magnetic sensors only while the map is open; these readings are used on the phone only, and are not stored or sent.

The app does not ask for background location permission and never tracks your location at other times.

The app gets your location from Google Play services' location service on your phone. Depending on your phone's location settings, that service may also use Wi-Fi and mobile networks to work out where you are; Google handles this under its own privacy policy. The app itself doesn't send your location anywhere.

While a trip records, Android shows an ongoing notification, and recording continues with the screen off or while you use other apps, until you finish the trip or Android stops the app. On Android 13 and later the app also asks for permission to show notifications, so that this notification can appear; recording works either way.

The notification has Mark spot and Finish buttons, and it also shows on your lock screen, so you can use them without unlocking your phone. It says only that a trip is recording and, for a few seconds after you mark a spot, whether the spot was saved. It never shows where you are. Anyone holding your phone while a trip records can see that it's recording, and can mark a spot or finish the trip from the lock screen.

For each trip, the app stores on your device:

  • when the trip started and finished, and its time zone;
  • its route: a series of GPS points, each with its time, latitude, longitude, accuracy, and altitude and speed when the phone reports them;
  • its distance;
  • its name, if you give it one;
  • whether it's a walk or a drive, if you change the type the app suggests;
  • the spots you mark while it records: for each spot, its number, time, location and height, and a name if you give it one.

Trips are used to show the route on a map, colored by speed; to show the trip's time, distance, moving time, top speed, climb and heights; to show your trip stats, with walks and drives counted separately; and to create GPX files when you ask for one.

All of this is worked out on your device. The app suggests whether a trip was a walk or a drive from its speed. On Android 14 and later, it converts the heights the phone reports into heights above sea level, using a model built into Android. Nothing is sent anywhere for this.

Maps and internet use

When you open a trip's screen or the app's map, the app loads the map from OpenFreeMap, a free map service. These requests ask for the map's tiles, fonts and icons for the area being shown, and carry the usual technical details of any internet request, such as your IP address. They do not contain your GPS points, but like any online map they show OpenFreeMap which area you're viewing. On the app's map, which follows where you are, that is usually the area around you. OpenFreeMap handles these requests under its own terms.

The map's hill shading, the soft shadows that show hills and valleys, comes from Mapterhorn, a free, open terrain-data service. These requests also show Mapterhorn the area you're viewing, in squares several kilometers across, and carry your IP address. They do not contain your GPS points. Mapterhorn keeps request logs, including IP addresses, for up to 30 days; see Mapterhorn's privacy policy.

Map and hill-shading tiles you've viewed are cached in the app's private storage, so maps load faster next time and areas you've viewed before can still show without internet.

Offline maps

You can download an offline map, for now of Israel, in Settings → Offline maps. The files come from GitHub (run by GitHub, Inc.), where the app's map files are published. The download runs in Android's download manager. As with any download, GitHub receives your IP address and the usual technical details of the request; no account, location, steps or trip data is sent. The app checks GitHub for the list of offline maps, to show their sizes and whether a newer one is out, only when you open Settings, and downloads a map only when you tap Download or Update. GitHub handles these requests under its own terms; see the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

A downloaded map is stored only on your device, in the app's own folder, and is removed when you delete it in Settings or uninstall the app. Inside its area, the map is drawn from it, with no requests to OpenFreeMap or Mapterhorn. OpenFreeMap, Mapterhorn, GitHub for offline maps, and Google's ads (see Ads) are the app's only internet use.

Ads

The app shows ads from Google AdMob in two kinds of places: a banner at the bottom of the Stats and History screens, and ad cards in your list of trips, each marked Ad. There are no ads on the Today, Map, trip and Settings screens, no full-screen ads, and no ad cards in the list of trips while a trip is recording.

The ads come from Google's Mobile Ads software inside the app. To show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, it sends Google your device's IP address (from which Google can estimate your general location, such as your city), your device's advertising ID and app set ID, how you interact with the ads (such as views and taps) and technical information about the app's performance. Google handles this under its own privacy policy; see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

  • What the ads never get. The app gives Google's ad software none of its own data: not your steps or anything else from Health Connect, not your trips, spots, imported routes or GPS location, and no keywords or other information about you. Ads are never chosen using your health or fitness data, as Google Play's Health Connect policy requires. Ads are limited to content rated for general audiences with parental guidance (PG).
  • Your choices. If you're in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app shows Google's consent form when you first open it, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy options for ads. Anywhere, you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalized ads, in your phone's Settings (Privacy → Ads, or Google → Ads, depending on the phone).

Sharing and saving GPX files

A trip leaves your device only when you share it: tap the Share icon at the top of the trip's screen (labelled "Share GPX" for screen readers), then choose where it goes. The GPX file contains the trip's route (the location, time and height of each point), the trip's name if it has one, and the spots you marked (the location, time, height and name of each). The file is named after the trip's date, and apps that use a subject line, such as email apps, also get "GPS route from" and that date.

Then you choose where it goes:

  • Save file saves the GPX file to a folder you pick. That folder may belong to another app or service, such as cloud storage, which handles the file under its own terms.
  • Any other choice, such as WhatsApp, Gmail or Drive, sends the file to that app. The app you choose, and the people you send it to, handle it under their own terms.

To share a file, the app writes a temporary copy to its private cache and gives read access only to the app you pick. Copies more than a day old are removed the next time you share a trip, and Android may clear the cache earlier on its own.

Routes you import (GPX files)

You can import a route someone planned, such as a hike or an off-road drive, from a GPX file, to see it on the app's map and follow it. The app reads a GPX file only when you choose to: when you open it with the app, share it to the app from another app, or pick it with Import GPX file on the map. It then gets access to that one file only, and needs no storage permission.

The file is read on your device, with no internet; nothing in it is sent anywhere, and the app doesn't follow any address written in it. From the file, the app stores on your device:

  • the route's name, and the file's name;
  • its tracks and routes: the location and, when the file has it, the height of each point;
  • its waypoints: the location, height, name and description of each;
  • when you imported it, whether it's shown on the map, and its distance and climb, worked out on your device.

The file itself isn't kept, and times and other details in it are ignored. Imported routes aren't trips: they don't show in your trips and don't count in your stats.

While the map is open, it uses the location it shows to say how far along a route you are and how far off it. This is worked out on your device as described under Location and GPS trips: it isn't stored or sent, and it stops when you leave the map.

Other information stored on your device

  • Your settings: step goal, the limits for telling workout steps from incidental steps, and whether deleted trips stay in Stats.
  • When steps were last updated, and the technical status of recent updates, such as whether they worked.
  • If you turn on Keep deleted trips in Stats: for each finished trip you delete, its date, duration, distance and whether it was a walk or a drive, so your trip totals keep counting it. No name, route, spots or location is kept.
  • Offline maps you download, the list of offline maps last read from GitHub, your Download over Wi-Fi only choice, and whether you answered the map's offer of an offline map. These say nothing about you beyond which region's map you downloaded.

All of this is kept in the app's private storage, which other apps can't read. It is not encrypted beyond Android's own protection of app storage.

How long data is kept, and deleting it

  • Trips and their spots are kept until you delete them in the app. Deleting a trip removes its name, route, GPS points and spots. You can also delete a single spot.
  • Imported routes are kept until you delete them in the app's list of routes. Deleting a route removes its points and waypoints; the GPX file it came from stays wherever it was.
  • Step counts and settings are kept until you uninstall the app or clear its storage in Android's settings, which also deletes your trips and imported routes.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored on your device. It does not delete step data in Health Connect, which Health Connect manages, or GPX files you saved, shared or imported from.

Backups and moving to a new phone

Android's cloud backup is turned off for this app, so nothing is backed up to your Google account. When you move to a new phone, Android's phone-to-phone transfer may still copy the app's data to the new phone.

Information shared with the developer or third parties

Apart from Google's ad software described under Ads, the app contains no advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting code. The app itself does not sell your personal information. The developer never receives your steps, trips or locations.

If you install the app from Google Play, Google provides the developer with statistics such as install counts and, if you agreed to share usage and diagnostics data with Google, crash and performance reports about the app. Google processes this data under its own privacy policy.

Estimates are for fun

Some numbers in the app are rough, for-fun estimates rather than measurements: calories and burgers burned, distance estimated from steps, and progress around the Earth and to the Moon. They should not be relied on for any purpose, including health decisions.

Children

The app is intended for a general audience and is not specifically designed for children. Ads in the app are limited to PG-rated content.
